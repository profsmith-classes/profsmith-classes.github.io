Course Description

How environmental and safety hazards influence public health and sustainability. Multiple perspectives on water, air, and solid waste pollution, sustainability and public safety issues.

This class is focused on providing you an introduction to the relationship between the environment and health; an introduction to environmental health; and, most importantly, an opportunity for you to reflect on your own values, learning, and goals in the context of your existence and the planet that you live on.

What an Asynchronous Online Course Means Here

This course is fully online and asynchronous. We will not meet in person or on Zoom as a class. Your progress through the course is self-paced within the weekly structure below. I generally do not post recorded lectures; instead you review the assigned materials, work through each module, and bring questions to me by email or in office hours.

General Education Writing Requirement

PUBH 115 is not a designated writing course and does not carry the General Education writing requirement.

You will nevertheless do a substantial amount of writing here. The Values Exercise and both Reflection Essays are written assignments, and each is assessed with the rubric attached to that assignment in Canvas. Writing quality - clarity, organization, and evidence that you engaged the course materials - is part of every one of those rubrics.

Course Student Learning Outcomes

On successful completion of this course, you will be able to:

  • Further your own critical self-understanding and acquire the knowledge, skills, and attitudes necessary to engage and reflect in learning and self-development practices.

  • Develop strategies to be an integrated physiological, socio-cultural, and psychological being, to promote a holistic awareness of lifelong learning throughout your life.

  • Develop yourself as a responsible citizen, employee and employer, family member, and member of the global society.

  • Actively apply and participate in developing a lifelong commitment to health for both personal well-being (physical, financial, emotional, intellectual, spiritual, social/interpersonal, and environmental) and societal responsibility.

  • Discuss concepts of sustainability associated with natural resources and human population.

  • Explore people's attitudes and motivations to change consumptive practices to preserve natural resources.

  • Characterize the health outcomes of exposure to environmental pollutants, including air, water, and food.

  • Understand the importance of safety in protecting the public and workers from various hazards at home and in workplaces.

  • Review careers available in environmental health and safety.

Policy on Generative Artificial Intelligence

You are strictly prohibited from using Generative AI (GenAI) or Large Language Models (LLMs) - such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and Grok - for research or writing assignments in this course. Any submitted work that has obviously used such tools will receive a failing grade. You are also prohibited from submitting or inputting any course material into a GenAI or LLM service, especially an online one.

The writing assignments in this course ask you to think about what you feel about a topic. They are designed for you to reflect on your own values. AI cannot perform this task.

Remember: in almost all cases, your writing will only ever be read by exactly two people - you and me.

Academic Integrity and Penalties for Academic Dishonesty

Most students hold themselves to high ethical standards. When others engage in academic misconduct, it can reduce morale and diminish the meaning of being a Titan. The following policy is intended to support everyone in the Titan community.

California State University has a strict policy against academic fraud and dishonesty. It is the student's responsibility to adhere to the guidelines regarding academic dishonesty as set forth in the university handbook. Policies can be found at the CSUF Dean of Students, Student Conduct policies and procedures. These include, but are not limited to, California Code of Regulations Title 5, Section 41301; Student Rights and Responsibilities (UPS 300.000); and the Academic Dishonesty Policy (UPS 300.021).

The success of public health intervention relies on the public's trust in the integrity of public health workers. Every act of dishonesty undermines the potential for public health officials and their research to be trusted. Probity should be considered a requisite characteristic of all persons working in the health sciences and is expected of Titans.

Penalties

All assignments in this course are individual, so all penalties below apply to the individual student. There is no group work and therefore no group penalty.

  • A student who inadvertently or negligently engages in academic dishonesty will be ineligible to receive credit for the related work - that is, a zero on that assignment.

  • The student may be reassessed on all course material using a variety of modalities, including oral examination.

  • Evidence of academic dishonesty will be reported to the Dean of Students and the CSUF Student Conduct team.

  • The student will receive an F in the course upon a determination that the incident was the result of willful intent or gross negligence.

  • Submitting work generated by GenAI or an LLM, in violation of the policy above, is academic dishonesty and is subject to these penalties.
